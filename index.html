Learning Passport
2026
The problem is not verification. It is meaning.

A qualification says where you studied. Not what you learned.

So every time a student crosses a border, a human being reads their transcript and guesses. Learning Passport makes the guess unnecessary.

01 — What the status quo costs

An administrator in Texas has no way of knowing what Matematika 1 in Serbia covered.

So the work is done by hand, one student at a time, and paid for twice — in fees, and again in credit that evaporates.

02 — The same desk, before and after

A course title tells an admissions officer nothing. So they estimate.

Three separate unknowns per subject — what the course actually covered, what the grade means, what it is worth — each one a judgment call by someone who does not read the language. With the curriculum already mapped, none of them is a judgment call.

Nothing about the student's paperwork changes. What changes is that the curriculum behind it has been read once — so the course opens into what it actually taught, the overlap with the receiving system is a measurement rather than an opinion, and where there is genuinely no equivalent the record says so instead of guessing.

06 — Why the economics work

They pay a human per student. We pay once per curriculum.

The incumbents

$150–300 per student — and an evaluator for every single one.

That is the shape of the entire credential-evaluation industry. Revenue per student is decent, but so is cost per student, and growth means hiring.

Learning Passport

Map a curriculum once. Every student who studied it is covered.

Serbian secondary mathematics is read, structured and matched a single time. After that, each student who carries it costs a lookup.

Revenue scales with students. Cost scales with curricula. There are only so many curricula in the world.

08 — For the technically minded

Curriculum as source code A curriculum document is parsed into its real structure, then reduced to the smallest independently teachable claims it makes — each one an action applied to an object under stated conditions.

Original language throughout Nothing is translated during extraction. Translating at that stage silently destroys the distinctions we exist to measure; cross-language meaning is carried as auditable metadata alongside the original, never in place of it.

Auditable by construction Every stage is isolated and every output stays traceable to the line it came from, so a wrong answer can be located rather than argued about — and any stage that can be deterministic code rather than a model call is code. That is what makes the output stable enough to score against a fixed answer key at all.

Matching Comparison across systems runs over those structured units in a shared semantic space rather than over course titles or free text, with genuinely ambiguous cases surfaced for expert review instead of being quietly resolved by a model.
